# Evidence brief: complementary founding teams

Research review: July 26, 2026.

## Practical conclusion

There is evidence that founding teams can benefit from different, legible
skills and structural roles. There is also strong reason not to reduce that
finding to "more difference is always better." Diversity can improve access to
knowledge and resources while also increasing coordination and conflict costs.

FindMate therefore treats complementarity as a hypothesis for a human
shortlist, not as a compatibility diagnosis:

> look for demonstrated capability gaps that another owner can cover, then
> separately test shared purpose and operating compatibility.

## What the research supports

| Finding | Evidence | What FindMate takes from it | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exploration and exploitation are different organizational learning activities. | [March, 1991](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2.1.71) | Distinguish creating new possibilities from refining known capabilities. | This is an organizational model, not a founder personality test or evidence for the exact `0→1 / 1→10 / 10→100` labels. |
| Founding-team skill diversity is associated with faster early growth, especially under uncertainty. | [D'Acunto, Tate, and Yang, U.S. Census working paper CES-20-45](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2020/adrm/CES-WP-20-45.html) | Ask what different capabilities an owner has actually demonstrated rather than searching for a clone. | The study measures pre-startup industry experience. Its 16% employment and 10% sales figures are associations from a working paper, not a promise of causal uplift for any pair. |
| Different network roles within an entrepreneurial team can be useful. | [Aven and Hillmann, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2017.2874) | Treat external relationships and partnership-building as a distinct contribution vector. | The evidence concerns brokering roles, starting capital, and historical Russian enterprises; it does not validate a general cofounder score. |
| Entrepreneurial-team diversity has mixed, context-dependent effects. | [Sundermeier and Mahlert, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.emj.2022.10.010) | Keep capability difference separate from shared goals, cohesion, and working norms. | The systematic review found a fragmented field and inconsistent outcomes across diversity dimensions and contexts. |
| Teams can benefit when members perceive both important similarity and useful difference. | [De Cooman et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/1059601115586910) | Match complementary skills while checking supplementary fit on principles and purpose. | The multi-wave study covered 121 people in 30 teams and was not a study of startup matching algorithms. |
| Teams coordinate expertise better when "who knows what" is legible. | [Lewis, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.88.4.587) | Publish bounded evidence for contribution vectors and preserve `unknown` when evidence is missing. | Transactive-memory research supports expertise coordination, not automated partner selection. |
| Diverse informational resources need integration processes and a supportive team climate. | [van Knippenberg, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-032516-113240) | Recommend a conversation and trial collaboration, not a verdict based only on profile distance. | This is a review of team innovation research, not a validated FindMate outcome study. |

## What the stage shorthand means

`0→1 / 1→10 / 10→100` is practitioner shorthand:

- `0→1`: problem discovery, invention, and a first working artifact;
- `1→10`: demand validation, iteration, and a repeatable early product or
  go-to-market loop;
- `10→100`: reliable systems, delegation, organizational scale, and economics.

The shorthand is useful for asking concrete questions, but it is not a
scientifically validated personality taxonomy. A person can contribute across
multiple stages, evidence can change over time, and missing evidence means
`unknown`, not inability.

## Protocol consequences

The evidence leads to six conservative rules:

1. assess demonstrated contributions, not titles or demographic traits;
2. keep stage contribution separate from functional contribution;
3. seek gap coverage, but require overlap in purpose and operating principles;
4. show evidence counts, confidence, and missing information;
5. recommend a small explainable shortlist for owner review;
6. require both humans to approve contact and test the relationship through a
   bounded collaboration before making a high-stakes commitment.

No paper above establishes that a FindMate score predicts founder success. The
protocol never claims psychometric validity, employment-screening validity, or
the ability to infer sensitive traits.

## How the hypothesis should be tested

FindMate currently has no independent owner-profile cohort and therefore no
outcome evidence. A future evaluation should be prospective and
consent-controlled:

- record only aggregate counts for owner-rated shortlist usefulness, mutual
  introduction opt-in, bounded trial starts, and voluntary 30/90-day
  continuation;
- predefine success and failure measures before evaluating outcomes;
- keep private drafts, identities, contact routes, messages, and rejected
  candidates out of repository telemetry;
- compare complementarity explanations with a simple transparent baseline;
- publish null and negative results, not only successful stories.

Until such evidence exists, FindMate is an open matching workflow with a
research-informed model—not a proven predictor of company formation or
performance.
